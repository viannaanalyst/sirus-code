# ADR-124: Phone app without tabs, with a settings sheet

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-13); the archiving it describes is superseded by [ADR-173](ADR-173-conversation-archive-removed.md).

## Context

The phone app (ADR-081) had a floating tab bar with Home, Projects and Settings. Settings was one page with the connection, alerts, usage and disconnect, and nothing about how the phone itself shows things. The owner asked for no tab bar: the home is the thread list, settings open as a sheet from a "···" button in the top corner, and the sheet lists grouped rows that each open a page (appearance, keyboard, organization, notifications, usage, about).

## Decision

- **Navigation.** The tab bar is gone. Home is the only base page. Its header has the brand and a round "···" button that opens Settings; an offline pill shows beside it only while the Mac is unreachable. A floating dock at the bottom holds Projects (left), search (middle) and a new conversation (right). Projects is now a page on the stack, with a back button. `MobileTab` was removed; `MobileScreen` gained `projects` and `settings`.
- **Settings sheet.** Settings is a stack sheet (`isSheet`) under a dimming scrim that closes it on tap. Rows are grouped as Connections (Mac status, Alerts, This device), Interface (Appearance, Keyboard), Projects and conversations (Organization, Archived conversations), and App (Usage, About Sirus). Each row opens its page inside the sheet, which slides in; the header shows a back button there and a close button on the index.
- **Phone-only preferences.** Appearance, keyboard and organization are kept in `localStorage` (`sirus.phone-prefs`, `src/lib/phone-prefs.ts`) and never change the Mac's settings. A missing value follows the Mac. They cover the color scheme (System, Light, Dark), the conversation text size (`uiFontSize`, 11–18), the terminal and code font sizes (a "Custom font size" switch, 10–18), wrapping long lines in code and diffs, the Return key (Send, or Add a line with Command-Return to send), and the project order (recent activity or name). `withPhonePrefs` lays them over the Mac's settings before `applyAppearance`; `usePhoneSetting` does the same for the terminal font size and diff wrapping; `returnAddsLine` is read by the shared composer. These apply only while the phone app is mounted (`active`), so the Mac window ignores them.

## Consequences

- Waiting conversations no longer have a badge on a Home tab; they stay first on Home under "Needs you".
- Usage, archived conversations and alerts still come from the Mac, as before.
- The phone app has no app version to show yet; About lists the Mac's shell, this device, the install hint and Disconnect.

## Addendum: snooze, app badge, working pill, touch review comments, queue order and prompt links

More of the phone app, as a home-screen web app (iPhone first).

- **Snooze from a row.** The swipe actions of a Home row are pin, snooze, archive and delete (four actions sit 8 px closer). Snooze opens a bottom sheet (`MobileSnoozeSheet` on `MobileActionSheet`) with 1 hour, this evening at 6 PM (offered while it is at least an hour away; `phoneSnoozeChoices` in `lib/snooze.ts`), tomorrow at 9 AM, next Monday at 9 AM, and a date and time. It calls the same `snoozeSession` as the Mac (ADR-103), so running or waiting conversations cannot be snoozed, a snoozed one leaves Home and search until it returns unread, and the undo toast (`SnoozeToast`) shows on the phone too. Home links to a Snoozed page (`MobileSnoozed`, soonest first, with a live countdown on each row) whose swipe actions are Return and Change.
- **App icon badge.** While the app is open, `AppIconBadge` keeps `navigator.setAppBadge` at the number of conversations that need the person (waiting, or with a pending approval or question; archived ones and side chats aside; `lib/phone-live.ts`) and clears it at zero. Session alerts from the Mac now carry that count (`badge` in the push payload, counted in `notifications::publish`; other alerts leave it out), and the service worker sets the badge from it before showing the alert. Everything is feature-detected; a refused or missing Badging API does nothing. On iOS the badge needs iOS 16.4 or later, the app on the Home Screen and alerts allowed. A conversation answered on the Mac while the phone app is closed sends no alert, so the badge can stay high until the app opens or the next alert.
- **Working pill.** While the agent runs, the chat shows a glass "Working · 42s" pill where the transcript's jump arrow sits (the arrow hides meanwhile). The time counts from the running turn's activity, less the time it waited on the person, or from the person's message before the reply starts. Its own one-second clock runs only while the page is visible. A tap follows the latest activity. It leaves when the turn ends or waits on the person; its pulse stops under reduced motion.
- **Review comments by touch.** The review already wraps the Mac's `ChangesPane` and `DiffViewer`, whose line numbers choose rows and whose comment is added to the conversation's draft as a quoted passage (`appendDiffComment`). On a coarse pointer, a tap on a line number chooses it, a tap on another extends the range, a tap on the only chosen line lets it go, and a tap once a range is chosen starts over (`nextDiffRange`); the hint says so. On the phone the rows are taller and the comment form rises as a sheet over the diff.
- **Queue order.** The composer's request queue gains move up and move down (`moveQueuedPrompt`, `lib/queue-order.ts`); a request that is sending or was already tried keeps the head of the line. The phone's offline outbox gets the same buttons (`moveQueued`, which moves a message among its conversation's messages only), next to cancel. Both have finger-sized buttons on the phone.
- **Prompt links.** Opening the app with `?prompt=…&project=…&provider=…&model=…` opens the new-conversation sheet with the prompt filled in and the project, provider and model chosen when they are here (`lib/phone-prompt-link.ts`). It never sends. The rules mirror ADR-122: a prompt is required and is at most 8,000 characters before invisible and bidi characters are removed (the same table as `prompt_link.rs`; the sheet warns when any were); each of the four parameters at most once; a project at most 1,024 characters without controls; a known provider; a model only with a provider and passing `launch::valid_model`. A refused link shows the Mac's reason as an error. Other parameters are left alone (the address also carries `session`), and the four are removed from the address at once. `repo` and `pr` are not read on the phone. On iOS, a link tapped elsewhere opens in Safari, which has its own storage and pairing, not in the Home Screen app.
